# Water and growing-season evidence

The application can calculate provisional historical FAO-56 ETo and, with an explicitly supplied crop-stage/Kc profile, standard-condition ETc. These are not on-farm observations, forecasts or planting prescriptions. Full water suitability and overall crop suitability remain unknown in the UI. [The final implementation report](FINAL_IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md) provides the exact implemented equations, supported crop/input table and current live verification results.

## NASA POWER inputs available in AgriLumina

The existing monthly point service requests T2M, PRECTOTCORR, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN and RH2M. Corrected precipitation is provided as a mean rate and converted to mm/month using each month's actual length. The Bekaa reference sums eight monthly totals; this is historical gridded **total precipitation**, not on-field measured rain, effective rainfall, irrigation or available soil water. Missing values produce unknown rather than zero. NASA documents its [monthly API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/monthly/) and [gridded meteorological sources](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_meteorology/).

When explicitly requested, a separate daily point request obtains T2M_MIN and T2M_MAX at 2 m with `time-standard=LST` for the Bekaa reference period. It checks every date, temperature units, missing/fill values and minimum/maximum ordering. The UI reports the coldest daily minimum, hottest daily maximum and count of days with a minimum at or below 0°C. This threshold describes the physical freezing point of water, **not** a validated crop-frost, soil-frost or crop-killing threshold. The hottest maximum has no invented crop heat-stress threshold. NASA's daily gridded estimates are not on-farm observations or a future forecast; local solar time is not the civil timezone. NASA's monthly API uses UTC by default, whereas this daily request uses local solar time; their day boundaries are not identical. See [NASA's daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) and [temporal processing](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/processing/).

An optional second daily NASA POWER request retrieves T2M_MIN, T2M_MAX, T2MDEW, WS2M, PS, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN and PRECTOTCORR, using local solar time. It validates all requested days, supported source units, fill values and basic physical consistency; errors are reported without fallback data. POWER's EVPTRNS is a modeled transpiration product, **not** automatically FAO reference evapotranspiration; see [NASA's parameter descriptions](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_hydrology/).

## FAO-56 equations and missing inputs

FAO-56 recommends the Penman–Monteith reference evapotranspiration equation:

```text
ETo = [0.408 Δ (Rn − G) + γ (900 / (T + 273)) u2 (es − ea)]
      / [Δ + γ (1 + 0.34 u2)]
ETc = Kc × ETo
```

ETo is reference evapotranspiration (mm/day), Δ is slope of the saturation-vapor-pressure curve, Rn is net radiation, G is soil heat flux, γ is the psychrometric constant, T is mean daily temperature, u2 is wind speed at 2 m, and es − ea is vapor-pressure deficit. The implementation uses daily G = 0, T = (Tmin + Tmax)/2, actual vapor pressure estimated from daily mean dew point, 2 m wind and gridded surface pressure. It estimates net radiation from NASA shortwave radiation, latitude/date and **farmer-entered elevation**, with the FAO grass albedo and longwave approximation. It caps the solar/clear-sky ratio at 1 with a warning and returns unknown below the supported cloudiness range rather than guessing. The daily dew-point conversion, gridded pressure, uniform reference surface and radiation estimation are approximations that need local validation. A missing elevation, invalid or incomplete climate, impossible vapor pressure, or unsupported radiation conditions yields an unknown day. A period total is withheld if any day is unknown. The calculation reproduces FAO-56 chapter 4 example 18 within rounding tolerance; see [FAO-56 reference ET](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e08.htm) and [meteorological equations](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e07.htm).

ETc is crop evapotranspiration under standard conditions. Kc depends on crop, planting date and initial, development, mid- and late-season stages; weather and wetting can require adjustment. These are not the same as actual stressed crop water consumption. See [FAO-56 crop coefficients](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0b.htm). The app stores the FAO winter wheat reference Kc ranges with their limitations, but does not auto-select a value or claim local validation. Farmers may enter a crop, planting date, four stage lengths, three Kc values and a source; these are user-supplied and not independently verified. The daily Kc curve linearly interpolates development and late stages. The Bekaa panel displays standard-condition wheat ETc only if the supplied wheat profile covers the entire period and every ETo day is available. Other crops can have stored profiles, but are not currently evaluated by that panel.

Total precipitation is not effective rainfall: runoff, evaporation, soil storage and deep percolation matter. FAO discusses [effective rainfall](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e03.htm) and simplified irrigation-need estimates, but their assumptions must be met. The field form accepts optional volumetric field capacity, wilting point, rooting depth, initial depletion, and dated actual or planned irrigation records with an explicit coverage period. It calculates total available water as `1000 × (field capacity − wilting point) × rooting depth` using volumetric fractions and metres, assuming uniform root-zone soil. An applied irrigation depth is not necessarily net infiltration. Blank records are unknown, not zero; an explicitly complete empty actual/planned schedule is a user assertion.

The pure `evaluateWaterBalance` function implements the FAO-56 daily root-zone equation `Dr = Dr_previous − (P − runoff) − net_irrigation − capillary_rise + actual_ET + deep_percolation` using **only supplied terms**. It requires a caller-supplied, sourced local allowable-depletion fraction before comparing depletion with a stress threshold, and rejects impossible storage instead of silently clipping. A manual one-day form in the water panel exposes this function using only values explicitly entered for that calculation; its inputs/results are not saved. It is not an automatic seasonal balance: actual ET, runoff, net infiltration, capillary rise, drainage and a locally reviewed threshold are not derived from the field or NASA data. It does not infer effective rainfall or irrigation efficiency. See [FAO-56 soil water stress](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0e.htm). No water-suitability score or overall ranking is fabricated.

## Locally sourced historical wheat reference

An explicitly selected Bekaa Valley context uses a [published Bekaa wheat study](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/7/2089), which describes cereals being sown in November and harvested the following June. The implementation requests two complete historical years, then evaluates November–December of the earlier year and January–June of the latter year. It applies the existing FAO ECOCROP pH/temperature screen to **durum wheat only**, and sums total precipitation for those months.

This is a **study-based historical reference period**, not a locally validated rule for the farmer's precise field, a verified durum cultivar calendar or an agronomic hard exclusion. A single broad species temperature range is applied to all eight months; the model does not represent crop stages or winter dormancy and could incorrectly exclude adapted winter wheat. The farmer selects the context; AgriLumina does not automatically verify the field's region. A Spring selection is identified as outside this study's November reference, not as proof that wheat cannot be grown. No other crop or location has a locally sourced period in this implementation. Users can retain the existing generic four-month screen, whose windows remain explicitly illustrative.

Daily climate calculations are not written to saved analyses. Optional farmer-entered water inputs are stored with the existing field record using the current local/Supabase workspace, without schema migrations. Existing records, authentication and policies are unchanged. Complete recommendations still require locally reviewed crop-specific calendars and rotation rules, full water-balance inputs and methods, local agronomic review, and independent validation.

## Recorded integration verification

On 12 November 2026 the live verifier received HTTP 200 for monthly 2025, daily temperature extremes for 1 November 2024-30 June 2025, and seven-parameter water climate for both that season and 1-3 July 2025 at public coordinates 33.85, 35.9. With a deliberately synthetic 1,000 m elevation, the three July ETo values sum to 20.839652983811547 mm. The full season has 242 complete input days but only 238 accepted ETo calculations: 28-29 December 2024, 7 March and 1 April 2025 have a solar/clear-sky ratio below 0.3. Seasonal ETo and ETc therefore remain unknown. This is a supported-range boundary, not a missing NASA response. A reviewed method for those conditions and the separate root-zone terms are still required.

The expanded synthetic account check verifies that all entered water/Kc/irrigation values survive saving/reload, plans/analysis snapshots and account switching without entering the guest workspace. It does not prove live Supabase persistence or RLS. See [the final report](FINAL_IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md) for exact live values and test boundaries and [README.md](README.md) for reproducible commands.
